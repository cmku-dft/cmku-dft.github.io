Research with atomic-scale clarity

Led by Pakpoom Reunchan at Kasetsart University, the Computational Materials Unit uses first-principles electronic-structure calculations and density-functional theory (DFT) to understand and design functional materials. Our research focuses on n-type and p-type transparent conducting oxides, defect physics in semiconductors, and hydrogen-related phenomena in materials. We also investigate two-dimensional and porous materials for gas sensing—particularly applications in agriculture and food safety—and for hydrogen storage.

Research

Questions that connect atoms to performance

We combine accurate electronic-structure calculations with materials insight to identify mechanisms and practical design rules.

01

Defects and doping

We investigate native point defects, impurities, and carrier doping in III–V, II–VI, and wide-band-gap semiconductors such as ZnO, In₂O₃, and SrTiO₃. Comparisons with X-ray and infrared spectroscopy help identify local defect structures and connect them to electrical and optical properties.

  • Point defects
  • Carrier doping
  • Spectroscopy
02

Hydrogen-storage materials

We explore hydrogen adsorption and storage in porous graphene, boron nitride, C₂N, and related low-dimensional materials. Chemical modification and mechanical strain provide routes for improving hydrogen binding and storage capacity.

  • Hydrogen storage
  • 2D materials
  • Materials tailoring
03

Beyond-lithium battery materials

We investigate carbon allotropes and two-dimensional layered materials as anodes for emerging rechargeable batteries beyond conventional lithium-ion technology. First-principles calculations reveal ion adsorption, diffusion pathways, storage capacity, voltage, and structural stability, with particular interest in sodium-ion systems.

  • Carbon allotropes
  • 2D anodes
  • Sodium-ion storage
04

Gas-sensing materials

First-principles studies of graphene-like materials, carbon-nitride-based systems, and MXenes for detecting gases relevant to agriculture and food safety, as well as toxic gases.

  • 2D materials
  • Food safety
  • Toxic gases

Thermodynamic and kinetic stability of Cl and F donors in ZnO

Principal Investigator

Pakpoom Reunchan

Pakpoom Reunchan is an Associate Professor of Physics at Kasetsart University and Principal Investigator of the Computational Materials Unit. He earned his Ph.D. in Physics from Suranaree University of Technology, where his doctoral research focused on first-principles studies of defect complexes in semiconductors. His international research experience includes a visiting appointment at the University of California, Santa Barbara, followed by postdoctoral fellowships at the Asia Pacific Center for Theoretical Physics in South Korea and the National Institute for Materials Science in Japan. His research applies density-functional theory and advanced first-principles methods to defect physics, transparent conducting oxides, hydrogen in materials, gas sensing, and hydrogen-storage materials.
